The route comes before the run, not after it

Most trackers hand you a map when you finish. Waypath starts there: draw the route you mean to take, follow it live, and let your own history build a heatmap of the paths you really use.

The interesting part is the disagreement. The heatmap shows where you actually go; the dashed line shows where you intended to. After a few weeks the gap between them is the most useful thing on the screen.

How a planned run works

  1. Draw it on the mapTap out the route before you leave. Distance updates as you draw, so you can shape it to the number you wanted.
  2. Follow it liveDuring the activity the route is on the map with your position on it, and you can see when you have drifted off.
  3. Keep itA route you liked is saved and can be run again, which is how a route becomes a benchmark rather than a one-off.
  4. Watch the heat buildEach completed activity feeds the heatmap. It gets useful somewhere around the tenth run.

What version one honestly is

The heatmap is yours alone

It is built entirely from your own recorded activities, on your own device. There is no community heatmap and no global one — the architecture has a place for it, but there is no backend behind that place, and shipping an empty social feature would be worse than not shipping it.

Which also means: nothing you record is uploaded anywhere. There is no account and no server. Your routes are a file on your phone.
